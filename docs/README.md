# OctoAcme Project Management Processes

Welcome to OctoAcme's centralized project management documentation. These guides define how the organization plans, executes, communicates, and improves work across projects so teams can move from idea to delivery with greater consistency and shared understanding.

## Process summary

OctoAcme follows a structured, phase-based project management lifecycle that starts with initiation, moves through planning and execution, and ends with release and retrospective. The organization emphasizes clear goals, measurable outcomes, and iterative delivery so teams can validate value early and adjust as needed. Each project is expected to define a problem statement, success metrics, milestones, dependencies, and risk considerations before moving into execution. This gives teams a common foundation while still allowing each project to adapt to business and technical realities.

The methodology also relies on distinctive roles and clear ownership. Product Managers define what should be built and why, Project Managers coordinate delivery, schedules, communication, and risk management, and developers implement features with quality and maintainability in mind. QA/testing validates acceptance criteria and release readiness, while stakeholders provide input and approvals. By combining these responsibilities into a cross-functional model, OctoAcme enables accountability without creating unnecessary handoff friction.

Communication is a core operating principle. The team uses regular check-ins such as weekly PM/Product Lead syncs, twice-weekly delivery standups, monthly stakeholder updates, and ad-hoc escalation when blockers or dependencies require attention. Status reporting centers on a single source of truth, and the organization maintains lightweight communication templates to keep everyone aligned on progress, risks, decisions, and next steps. In parallel, risk management uses a register and escalation path to surface issues early and drive resolution before they become business-impacting problems.

Quality assurance and continuous improvement are embedded throughout the process. Teams are expected to use pull request reviews, CI checks, automated tests, security scanning, smoke tests, and manual QA when needed to protect release quality. After each sprint or milestone, the team reviews what went well, what needs to change, and which actions should be carried forward. This creates a learning loop that helps OctoAcme refine its process over time, reduce avoidable friction, and scale institutional knowledge across the organization.
